Slope coefficients and group deviations use reference 0; multipliers use reference 1. Absolute locations, thresholds, log gaps and variance shares have no directional reference. A dash means no reference is defined. Table probabilities are model-conditional, not p-values. Displayed results use two decimal places; calculations and JSON exports retain full numerical precision. Warning thresholds use unrounded diagnostics, so rounding can mask a boundary (for example, R-hat may display 1.01 and still be flagged).

How to read the checks

We flag classical split R-hat above 1.01 and approximate effective sample size (ESS) below 400. Undefined, nonfinite or frozen streams also trigger warnings. These are practical checks, not proof of convergence or model adequacy. No posterior predictive check or test of proportional odds is implemented. Common residual variance and exchangeable group deviations are model assumptions, not conclusions from these checks. This R-hat is not rank-normalized; ESS uses an initial-positive-pair autocorrelation estimate.

Exact conjugate draws are independent by construction; their diagnostics provide an additional implementation check. Logistic and ordinal MALA adapt their step sizes only during discarded warmup. Hierarchical blocked Gibbs discards warmup sweeps and requires no proposal tuning. All four streams have different deterministic seeds.

Income comparison and ordinary income regression use Normal–Inverse-Gamma priors; their exponentiated coefficients have no finite posterior means. Hierarchical income uses different independent priors and reports natural income medians by presentation choice. Intervals concern model parameters, not individual employees’ future outcomes.

A DEMONSTRATION, WITH CONTEXT

A familiar dataset.
A fictional workforce.

The IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance dataset contains 1,470 fictional employees and 237 Attrition = Yes records. IBM data scientists created it for demonstration. It is not a sample of actual IBM employees.

There is no documented attrition observation window, so these proportions are not annual turnover rates. Monthly income is in unspecified income units/month. No currency is assumed.

Try a smaller department to see how the prior and sample size affect uncertainty. The models describe associations in this dataset; they do not establish causes or provide a validated operational prediction system.
